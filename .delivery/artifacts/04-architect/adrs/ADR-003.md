# ADR-003: Pipeline Stage Count -- 8 Stages vs Merged Alternatives

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-04-12
**Deciders:** Solution Architect (Celebrimbor)
**Pipeline:** run-2026-04-12-hw01 | **Type:** GREENFIELD

---

> "Eight stages, like the eight points of a star upon a great ring. Each stage serves a distinct purpose in the forging of hardware. To merge them is to weaken the metal -- to conflate design with manufacture is to invite the flaws that brought ruin to lesser works."

---

## Context

The PRD specifies 8 pipeline stages for hardware development:

1. **Concept** -- Requirements, constraints, feasibility
2. **Schematic** -- Circuit design, component selection, simulation
3. **Layout** -- PCB physical design, routing, DRC
4. **Prototype** -- Physical prototype fabrication and bring-up (human-execution)
5. **DFM/DFA** -- Design for manufacturability/assembly review
6. **Compliance** -- EMC, safety, environmental regulatory review
7. **Pilot Run** -- Small production run validation (human-execution)
8. **Production Release** -- Full production handoff (human-execution)

The advisory challenge C3 raised concerns that 8 stages may be over-decomposed for AI context, particularly since 3 stages involve human physical execution. The question is whether 8 stages is the right count or whether merging some stages would be more effective.

## Decision

**Keep 8 stages.** Each stage has a distinct purpose, distinct primary role(s), distinct gate(s), and distinct outputs. The stages map 1:1 to recognized phases of real-world hardware product development.

**Key design elements that make 8 stages viable:**

1. **Execution mode classification**: Stages are classified as AI-execution (5 stages) or human-execution (3 stages). This makes the pipeline honest about what AI can and cannot do -- physical fabrication, assembly, and testing require human hands.

2. **Sub-agent dispatch**: Each stage is dispatched as a sub-agent via the Agent tool. The orchestrator's context does not grow linearly with stage count because sub-agents have isolated context. The orchestrator tracks stages via state metadata, not by accumulating all stage content.

3. **Phase 2 routing matrix**: For simpler projects (hobby, prototype), stages can be minimized or skipped. A 1-layer prototype may skip Compliance and Pilot Run entirely, effectively running as a 5-6 stage pipeline. This is Phase 2 (FR-021) but the architecture supports it.

4. **Rework paths are stage-specific**: The 6 defined rework paths reference specific stage pairs (e.g., DFM/DFA --> Layout). Merging stages would make rework targeting ambiguous -- "DFM found a layout issue" is clear; "Manufacturing Review found an issue in the Design stage" requires sub-stage disambiguation.

## Consequences

### What becomes easier

- **Clear responsibility**: Each stage has one primary role. No ambiguity about who does what.
- **Precise rework targeting**: Rework jumps to a specific stage, not a vague phase.
- **Gate granularity**: Each gate tests a specific quality dimension (DRC, BOM, DFM, compliance). Merged gates would need to handle heterogeneous validation.
- **Human-execution clarity**: Physical stages are explicitly marked and follow a distinct orchestration pattern (gate-in/human-action/gate-out).
- **Progress tracking**: 8 discrete checkpoints make pipeline progress visible and resumable.

### What becomes harder

- **Perceived complexity**: 8 stages looks heavyweight for a simple prototype project. Mitigated by Phase 2 stage routing (skip/minimize for simple projects).
- **Context management**: The orchestrator must track 8 stages of state. Mitigated by sub-agent dispatch (each stage's content is isolated in a sub-agent).
- **Full pipeline duration**: A full 8-stage run takes longer than a merged 5-stage alternative. Mitigated by human-execution stages being natural pause points (pipeline saves state).

## Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Pros | Cons | Why Rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 stages: Design (Concept+Schematic+Layout), Prototype, Review (DFM+Compliance), Pilot, Production | Fewer stages; simpler orchestrator | Conflates schematic and layout (different roles, different skills); merges DFM and compliance (different expertise, different gates); loses rework precision | Over-merging loses the engineering discipline boundaries that make gates effective |
| 6 stages: Concept, Design (Schematic+Layout), Prototype, Validation (DFM+Compliance), Pilot, Production | Compromise; slightly fewer stages | Still conflates schematic and layout; "Design" stage would need two primary roles (EE and PCB Layout) in the same sub-agent, breaking context isolation | The EE and PCB Layout roles have fundamentally different references and expertise |
| 10 stages: add Firmware and Mechanical as separate stages | Covers more domains | Firmware and Mechanical are Phase 2 roles; adding their stages without role implementations creates empty pipeline steps | Premature; add stages when the roles are implemented |
| Variable stages per project type (3 for hobby, 8 for production) | Lean for simple projects | Complex orchestrator logic; Phase 1 scope creep; complicates state management | Deferred to Phase 2 (FR-021) as stage routing, not stage elimination |
