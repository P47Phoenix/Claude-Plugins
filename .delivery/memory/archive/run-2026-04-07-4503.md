## What Went Well

- **Bundling was wisdom.** One pipeline against four kindred issues spared us three or four sequential runs all editing the same files. The sword struck once and true.
- **Adversarial review at Refine earned its keep.** Three significant gaps were caught before a single line of implementation: C1 force_type override, C3 Bash bypass, and C4 loop non-convergence. Better to bleed in council than in battle.
- **Light-mode Architect held its shape.** Three ADRs, no more, no less — enough light to see the path without slowing the march.
- **Fresh QA evaluator each round.** The Isolated Adversarial Loop pattern proved itself in practice — independent eyes each round, no creeping familiarity.

## What Did Not Go Well

- **Wizard question count math was wrong twice.** Gimli assumed the number rather than counting it. Twice. The QA evaluator caught it both times, but two rounds of dev rework could have been one — or none — had we counted from the start.
- **Dev stage first-try pass rate dipped.** Three self-correction rounds where one would have served. The wizard numbering issue ate two of those rounds; one stale reference in SKILL.md ate the third.
- **The orchestrator nearly broke its own rule.** Reaching to fix SKILL.md line 1051 directly instead of delegating — caught in the moment and corrected, but meta-proof that the anti-pattern is real and ever-present. The discipline this bundle was built to enforce was nearly violated by the very hand building it. Mark this well.

## Lessons Carried Forward

- Bundling related issues into a single pipeline run is the right move when artifacts overlap. Make it a default consideration when triaging the backlog.
- Adversarial review pays its rent at Refine; do not be tempted to skip it on "small" features.
- The Isolated Adversarial Loop pattern works. Keep evaluators fresh, always.
- The orchestrator must delegate, even when the fix is one line away. Especially then.
