# Oral Delivery & Pacing

On demand only. This check is **not** part of the pre-delivery gate.

## Purpose

Review the sermon as a spoken piece, not just a written one.

## Checks For

- Sentence length and rhythm suited to spoken delivery
- Clear structural signposting (intro, movements, conclusion) that a listener can follow without seeing text
- Appropriate pacing — where to slow down, where a pause matters
- Balance between exposition, illustration, and application
- Length appropriateness for the stated context (if given)

## Output Format

A list of delivery notes. The fields are defined in `contracts/delivery-notes.md`.

## Standards

- Focus on deliverability, not theological content — leave doctrinal and factual concerns to the other agents.
- Suggestions should be concrete enough to apply directly (not just "tighten this up").

## How to Check

1. Read it aloud in your head at about 130 words per minute; estimate the spoken length and compare it to the stated target
2. Mark sentences over about 25 words or with nested clauses, since a listener cannot re-read them
3. Check that each movement is announced and that the listener could say where they are
4. Mark places for a pause (after a key claim, after a long verse reading)
5. Count how much of each movement is exposition, illustration and application, and say where it is lopsided
6. Check scripture readings: are long readings broken up, and is each reference spoken in a way a listener can follow?
7. Check repeated words and lists for sound, such as three items in a row that rhyme or collide
8. Give a specific rewrite for each issue; do not change doctrine or facts. If a fact looks wrong, send it to the other checks and say so in one line
