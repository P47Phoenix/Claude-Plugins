# Plain-Language Style

Write for a general reader, not a theologian. This never overrides accuracy or sourcing: simplify the wording, not the substance.

## Rules

1. **Gloss jargon on first use.** If a technical or original-language term is necessary, explain it in plain words the first time it appears. Examples to gloss: *huiothesia* ("adoption as a son"), *supersessionism* ("the view that the church replaces Israel"), *hermeneutic* ("a way of reading"), *typology* ("a pattern where an earlier person or event points ahead")
2. **Keep sentences short and direct.** One idea per sentence. Aim for under 25 words
3. **Prefer everyday words.** "Use" over "utilize"; "show" over "demonstrate"
4. **Put the point first.** Then the evidence
5. **Name things.** Use the person, book or verse, not "this" or "it" at a distance
6. **Use active voice** unless the actor is unknown or unimportant
7. **No filler.** Cut "it is important to note that"
8. **Keep original-language terms few.** Only the ones the argument needs, each given its transliteration and sense

## Glossing Pattern

> The Greek word is *monē* (a place to stay).

> Scholars call this a **type**: an earlier person or event that points ahead to something later.

Use bold for a term you are defining, italics for a transliterated word.

## What Not to Simplify

- Do not drop a citation to make a sentence shorter
- Do not turn a contested point into a settled one
- Do not turn an echo into a quotation for the sake of a smoother line
- Do not state a lexical sense you did not check

## Layout

- Short headings that say what the section does
- Bullets for lists of parallel items; prose for argument
- Tables only for comparisons
- Show verse references as Book chapter:verse (John 14:23)

## Self-Check Before Returning

1. Could someone with no seminary training follow it?
2. Is every Greek or Hebrew word glossed and transliterated?
3. Is every sentence short enough to say in one breath?
4. Is any accuracy lost? If so, put the substance back and simplify the wording another way
