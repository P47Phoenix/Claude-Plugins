# Jewish Sources: Mishnah, Talmud, Midrash

How to cite Jewish oral tradition without vagueness or overreach.

## The Rule

When Jewish oral tradition is invoked, cite the specific source: the tractate and folio in the Talmud, or the specific Midrash. **Never write "Jewish tradition says" or "the rabbis taught"** without naming the text.

## What the Sources Are (gloss on first use)

- **Mishnah:** the earliest written collection of Jewish oral law, edited around AD 200. Cited by tractate, chapter and mishnah number (e.g. Mishnah Sukkah 4:9)
- **Talmud:** the Mishnah plus later discussion (the Gemara). Two versions: the **Babylonian Talmud** (Bavli, cited "b." by tractate and folio, e.g. b. Berakhot 33b) and the **Jerusalem Talmud** (Yerushalmi, cited "y.", usually by tractate, chapter and halakhah, e.g. y. Hagigah 2:1)
- **Midrash:** rabbinic commentary on Scripture. Cite by the named work and section (e.g. Bereishit Rabbah 33:6; Mekhilta de-Rabbi Yishmael on Exodus; Sifre Deuteronomy; Vayikra Rabbah)
- **Tosefta, Targums, Pirkei Avot** and others: cite by their own names and divisions

## Citation Format

| Source | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mishnah | Mishnah Tractate chapter:mishnah | Mishnah Chagigah 2:1 |
| Babylonian Talmud | b. Tractate folio+side | b. Sanhedrin 108b |
| Jerusalem Talmud | y. Tractate chapter:halakhah | y. Hagigah 2:1 |
| Midrash Rabbah | Work chapter:section | Bereishit Rabbah 33:6 |
| Pirkei Avot | Pirkei Avot chapter:mishnah | Pirkei Avot 3:2 |

Folios have two sides: **a** (front) and **b** (back). Never give a folio without the side.

## Linking to Sefaria

Sefaria (sefaria.org) publishes these texts with English translation and is a good place for the user to check a citation.

- Link form is `https://www.sefaria.org/<Tractate>.<folio>` for the Bavli (e.g. `Berakhot.33b`) and `https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_<Tractate>.<chapter>.<mishnah>` for the Mishnah
- **Fetch and read the page before you include a link.** If it does not resolve or does not contain the passage, do not include the link; keep the plain citation
- Sefaria's section numbering for Midrash Rabbah can differ from print editions. State the edition or note "Sefaria numbering" when it matters

## Dating and Use (anachronism check)

- The Mishnah (about AD 200), the Talmuds (about AD 400-600) and the Midrashim (various, many after AD 400) were written **after** the New Testament. Do not present a later rabbinic text as proof of a first-century practice
- Phrase it as "a later text, b. X, describes...", not "in Jesus' day they..." unless the Mishnah itself, or a first-century source such as Josephus or Philo, supports the dating
- Where a custom is attested only in later literature, say so; where a source describes a Temple practice (e.g. Mishnah Sukkah on the water ceremony), say that it records an earlier practice in later form

## Do Not Overreach

- Rabbinic literature records disagreement. Cite the view, name who held it if the text does, and say others disagreed where they did
- Do not claim that a Jewish source "teaches the same as the Gospel." Say what it says; let the parallel be labeled **echo** or **parallel**
- Do not misrepresent Jewish theology or practice. If you are unsure how a text is understood within Judaism, say you are not certain, or leave it out
- Do not use a Jewish source you cannot locate. Mark it unverifiable and remove it

## Folk Claims to Avoid

Do not repeat popular claims about Jewish custom unless a primary source supports them. A prominent example is the "groom prepares a room and returns for the bride" reading of John 14:2-3. See `viral-claims.md` in `topic-verification`; it is currently unsourced and should be left out.

## When You Cannot Cite It

- Remove the claim; do not write "tradition says"
- List it under "Gaps" in the internal draft so the gate can report "Removed: [claim], no citable source"
