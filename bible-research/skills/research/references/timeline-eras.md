# Timeline Eras

Loaded for `timeline` and `song-ideas`. The router pastes the "Era pick list" and "Dating rules" sections into every researcher, and only the chosen era's section from "Event leads". Event leads are **leads by passage reference, to be opened and checked at use**; the dates below are the ranges a named chronology gives and must be re-verified against that work (or another scholarly chronology) before they appear in output. Nothing here is a citation on its own.

## Dating rules

1. **Every date is a range or `[CONTESTED]`**, with the chronology named (guardrail 12). A single year appears only when tied to a named external anchor (rule 2) with a source ID; never a bare year from memory
2. **Regnal and relative dates come from the text; absolute dates come from a chronology** that anchors them to a fixed point (the Assyrian eponym list and the eclipse of 763 BC; the Babylonian Chronicle for 597 BC; the Battle of Carchemish 605 BC; the death of Herod the Great, itself contested, rule 4; the fifteenth year of Tiberius, Luke 3:1). Name the anchor when giving an absolute date
3. **Eras 1-2 carry no absolute dates.** Primeval and patriarchal events are placed by sequence and by the text's own spans (for example Genesis 15:13 and Exodus 12:40-41), and any absolute date is `[CONTESTED]` with the positions named (for example an early second-millennium patriarchal setting, Kitchen, versus a later setting or a literary reading, Thompson, Van Seters). Do not print a creation date
4. **Known splits that must show both positions:** the Exodus (early date, fifteenth century BC, on 1 Kings 6:1 read literally; late date, thirteenth century BC, on Exodus 1:11 and the archaeology; Kitchen holds late, Wood and Merrill hold early); the fall of Jerusalem (587 or 586 BC; Thiele holds 586, Young 587; the split turns on Tishri versus Nisan years and on accession versus non-accession reckoning); the birth of Jesus (a range before Herod's death, itself placed in 4 BC by most, 1 BC by a minority); the crucifixion (AD 30 or 33; Hoehner holds 33, Humphreys and Waddington argue 33, many commentators hold 30); Paul's chronology (anchored on the Gallio inscription, proconsul in Achaia around AD 51-52)
5. **A date with no located source is dropped**, not estimated
6. **State the calendar** when it matters (Nisan or Tishri year; Julian for anything before 1582)
7. **Order before dates.** A timeline must get the sequence right from the text even where no absolute date survives. Mark "sequence only" where that is all the text gives

## Event leads by era

Each line gives the event and the passage(s) to open. Where a chronology range is given, name the work in the output and open it or a page that cites it by section. These are starting points, not an exhaustive list; add events the text clearly marks and drop any you cannot source.

### 1. Primeval history
Creation (Genesis 1-2); the fall (Genesis 3); Cain and Abel (Genesis 4); the flood (Genesis 6-9); the table of nations and Babel (Genesis 10-11). No absolute dates (rule 3). Sequence only.

### 2. The patriarchs
Call of Abram (Genesis 12); the covenant (Genesis 15, 17); Sodom (Genesis 19); Isaac born (Genesis 21); the binding of Isaac (Genesis 22); Jacob and Esau (Genesis 25-33); Joseph sold, Joseph in Egypt (Genesis 37-41); the family goes down to Egypt (Genesis 46). Absolute setting `[CONTESTED]` (rule 3); the text's own spans: 430 years in Egypt (Exodus 12:40-41; the LXX reads the span differently, note it), 400 years of affliction (Genesis 15:13).

### 3. Exodus and wilderness
Moses born, the burning bush (Exodus 2-3); the plagues and Passover (Exodus 7-12); the sea crossing (Exodus 14); Sinai and the covenant (Exodus 19-24); the golden calf (Exodus 32); the tabernacle (Exodus 35-40); the spies and the forty years (Numbers 13-14); Moses' death (Deuteronomy 34). The Exodus date is the standard split (rule 4): early, fifteenth century BC; late, thirteenth century BC.

### 4. Conquest and judges
Jordan crossing and Jericho (Joshua 3-6); the land divided (Joshua 13-21); the cycle of the judges (Judges 2:11-19); Deborah (Judges 4-5); Gideon (Judges 6-8); Jephthah (Judges 11), with the 300-year note at Judges 11:26; Samson (Judges 13-16); Ruth (Ruth 1-4, "in the days when the judges ruled"). The period's length depends on the Exodus date; give the range both ways.

### 5. The united monarchy
Samuel (1 Samuel 1-8); Saul anointed (1 Samuel 9-10); David anointed, Goliath (1 Samuel 16-17); David king over Judah then all Israel (2 Samuel 2, 5); the ark to Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6); the Davidic covenant (2 Samuel 7); Bathsheba (2 Samuel 11-12); Absalom (2 Samuel 15-18); Solomon king (1 Kings 1-2); the temple begun in Solomon's fourth year (1 Kings 6:1) and dedicated (1 Kings 8); Solomon's decline (1 Kings 11). Chronologies place Saul, David and Solomon across roughly the eleventh and tenth centuries BC; the regnal lengths are in the text (2 Samuel 5:4-5; 1 Kings 11:42); absolute anchors come from the divided-kingdom synchronisms worked back (Thiele; Kitchen). Give ranges, name the work.

### 6. The divided kingdom
The split under Rehoboam and Jeroboam (1 Kings 12); Shishak's campaign (1 Kings 14:25-26; the Karnak relief is an external witness); Omri and Ahab (1 Kings 16); Elijah on Carmel (1 Kings 18); Elisha (2 Kings 2-13); Jehu (2 Kings 9-10; the Black Obelisk is an external witness); Amos and Hosea in the north, Isaiah and Micah in the south (the books' opening verses give the kings); the fall of Samaria (2 Kings 17), dated by the Assyrian records to the late 720s BC (name the chronology). Thiele's system is the standard reference for the regnal synchronisms; note where others (Galil, Young) differ.

### 7. Judah alone and the exile
Hezekiah and Sennacherib's siege (2 Kings 18-19; Isaiah 36-37; Sennacherib's prism is an external witness, usually 701 BC); Manasseh (2 Kings 21); Josiah's reform and the book found (2 Kings 22-23); Josiah's death at Megiddo (2 Kings 23:29); Carchemish (Jeremiah 46:2; 605 BC by the Babylonian Chronicle); the first deportation with Daniel (Daniel 1:1-6); Jehoiachin's exile (2 Kings 24:10-17; 597 BC by the Babylonian Chronicle); the fall of Jerusalem and the temple burned (2 Kings 25; Jeremiah 52), `[CONTESTED]` 587 or 586 BC (rule 4); Ezekiel by the Chebar canal (Ezekiel 1:1-3); Lamentations; Jehoiachin released (2 Kings 25:27-30).

### 8. Return and Second Temple
The decree of Cyrus (Ezra 1:1-4; 2 Chronicles 36:22-23; the Cyrus Cylinder is an external witness, though it does not name Judah), first year of Cyrus over Babylon; the altar and foundation (Ezra 3); work stopped and resumed under Haggai and Zechariah (Ezra 4-6; Haggai 1:1 dates to Darius's second year); the second temple completed in Darius's sixth year (Ezra 6:15); Esther under Ahasuerus (Esther 1:1; usually identified with Xerxes I, note the identification); Ezra's return in Artaxerxes' seventh year (Ezra 7:7-8); Nehemiah and the walls in Artaxerxes' twentieth year (Nehemiah 2:1); Malachi. Whether Ezra came before Nehemiah is a known scholarly question; note it if it comes up.

### 9. Between the Testaments
Alexander's conquest (Daniel 8 and 11, commonly read as prophecy of this period, dating of Daniel `[CONTESTED]`; 1 Maccabees 1:1-9 as history); the Ptolemies and Seleucids; Antiochus IV and the desecration of the temple (1 Maccabees 1:20-64; Daniel 11:31 as commonly read); the Maccabean revolt and the rededication (1 Maccabees 4:36-59; the origin of Hanukkah, John 10:22 mentions the feast); the Hasmoneans; Pompey takes Jerusalem (Josephus, *Antiquities* 14); Herod the Great made king by Rome (Josephus, *Antiquities* 14-15). Protestant readers treat 1-2 Maccabees as history, not scripture; say so once. Dates in this era are well anchored in Greek and Roman records; still give the source.

### 10. The life of Jesus
Birth under Herod the Great (Matthew 2:1; Luke 2:1-7; Herod's death is the anchor, rule 4); the twelve-year-old in the temple (Luke 2:41-52); John the Baptist and the baptism, dated by Luke 3:1-2 (the fifteenth year of Tiberius); the temptation (Matthew 4); the ministry, with the Passovers of John 2:13, 6:4 and 11:55 as the usual basis for a two-to-three-year span (note that this is a reconstruction); the transfiguration (Matthew 17:1-8; Mark 9:2-8; Luke 9:28-36); the triumphal entry (all four Gospels); the Last Supper (Matthew 26; Mark 14; Luke 22; John 13), with the Synoptic-John dating question flagged `[CONTESTED]`; the crucifixion `[CONTESTED]` AD 30 or 33 (rule 4); the resurrection (Matthew 28; Mark 16; Luke 24; John 20-21); the ascension (Acts 1:1-11).

### 11. The early church
Pentecost (Acts 2); Stephen (Acts 7); Saul's conversion (Acts 9; Galatians 1); Cornelius (Acts 10); Antioch (Acts 11); James killed, Peter freed, Herod Agrippa I dies (Acts 12; Josephus places Agrippa's death in AD 44, *Antiquities* 19); the first journey (Acts 13-14); the Jerusalem council (Acts 15; Galatians 2 is often linked, flag the link as a scholarly question); the second journey, Corinth and Gallio (Acts 18:12; the Gallio inscription anchors around AD 51-52); the third journey, Ephesus (Acts 19); arrest in Jerusalem, Caesarea, the voyage to Rome (Acts 21-28); the letters, placed by their own internal markers and the Acts framework (give the usual ranges and name the introduction used); the fire of Rome and Nero (Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44, for the persecution); the fall of Jerusalem in AD 70 (Josephus, *War* 6); John on Patmos (Revelation 1:9; Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.30.3, for the Domitianic date, `[TRADITIONAL]` and `[CONTESTED]` against an earlier date).

## What to produce per era

For each event: name, passage reference(s), the relative placement (what came before and after, from the text), the date range or `[CONTESTED]` positions with source IDs, any external witness, and one plain-language sentence on why the event matters in the story. Leave out an event you could not open in the text.
