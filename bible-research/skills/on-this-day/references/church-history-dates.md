# Church-History Dates

How to find and vet a documented event in church history that is tied to a specific calendar date.

## The Rule

Include an event only if there is a **dated, citable historical source**. Do not include undated or loosely dated traditions as if they were fixed to this day. If no dated, sourced event exists for the date, say so.

## What Counts as Documented

| Counts | Does not count |
|---|---|
| A council opened, decided or closed on a recorded date (cite the acts or a standard history) | "Tradition holds that X happened around this time" |
| A reformer's documented act, such as a letter, a speech, a published work with a dated colophon | A date first found in a devotional or blog with no source |
| A translation milestone with a dated printing or a documented completion | A saint's feast day used as if it were the date of the historical event |
| A trial, execution or death recorded in a dated court or chronicle record | A round year ("in 1517") presented as a particular day |

## Vetting Steps

1. **Find the event** and the date claimed
2. **Find the source for the date**: a primary document (the letter, the bull, the council acts), a scholarly history, or a standard reference (Oxford Dictionary of the Christian Church, a university press history, a denominational archive)
3. **Check for disputes.** Many famous dates are traditional and debated. If scholars dispute the date or the details, include the event with the dispute stated, and label it **contested**
4. **Check the calendar.** A date before 1582 in the West, or before 1752 in Britain and its colonies, is in the **Julian calendar**. Say which calendar the source uses, and give the Gregorian equivalent only if you can cite a conversion. Many sources use "Old Style" (OS) and "New Style" (NS)
5. **Check the year.** Some sources start the year on 25 March. Confirm before stating a January to March date
6. **Name the source** with author, title and year, or the primary document by name
7. **Mark the confidence:** documented / traditional / contested

## Leads (to re-verify at use; not citations)

These are leads only. Open a source for each before reporting it. Do not copy the date from this list.

| Date (as commonly given) | Event | Source to open |
|---|---|---|
| 31 October 1517 | Luther's letter to Archbishop Albert of Mainz enclosing the Ninety-Five Theses. The traditional story of nailing them to the Wittenberg church door is **disputed** by historians | A scholarly Luther biography or the letter itself in the Luther's Works edition |
| 17-18 April 1521 | Luther's hearing at the Diet of Worms | A scholarly history of the Reformation; the Diet records |
| 25 June 1530 | The Augsburg Confession presented to Emperor Charles V | The Book of Concord (Kolb and Wengert edition) |
| 27 September 1540 | Papal bull confirming the Society of Jesus | The text of the bull and a scholarly Jesuit history |
| 13 December 1545 | The Council of Trent opens | The acts of the council or a standard history of Trent |

Every entry needs its own source before it goes in a report.

## Entry Format

```
Date: [DAY MONTH YEAR] ([Julian | Gregorian; Old Style | New Style if relevant])
Event: [WHAT HAPPENED, ONE SENTENCE]
Confidence: [documented / traditional / contested]
Source: [AUTHOR, TITLE, YEAR; OR PRIMARY DOCUMENT NAME]
Note: [DISPUTES, CALENDAR ISSUES, WHAT COULD NOT BE CONFIRMED]
```

## Plain-Language Pattern

> On 25 June 1530 the Augsburg Confession, a statement of Lutheran belief, was read to Emperor Charles V. (Source: the Book of Concord.) The date is documented.

## Do Not

- Merge a feast day and a historical event under one date without saying which is which
- Say "on this day" for an event known only to the year or the month
- Present a legend as fact. Label it as legend or tradition and give the earliest source
- Use a date you could not find a source for
