Here are five places in Ezekiel that point toward Jesus. Pick one and I'll break it down in full.

## 1. The Good Shepherd: Ezekiel 34

**Why it fits:** God condemns Israel's leaders for feeding themselves and not the flock (34:2–4). Then he says, "I myself will search for my sheep" (34:11). He also promises "one shepherd, my servant David" (34:23–24). Jesus takes up this picture in John 10:11–16, where he calls himself the good shepherd.
**Textual note:** In 34:16 God says he will "seek the lost." Jesus echoes this in Luke 19:10: "the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost." The Hebrew for shepherd is ro'eh. In the ancient Near East it was also a common title for kings.

## 2. The Valley of Dry Bones: Ezekiel 37:1–14 (with 37:24–28)

**Why it fits:** A nation that is dead and without hope is brought back to life by God's breath. The chapter goes on to promise one shepherd-king and God's dwelling among his people (37:27). John 1:14 picks this up: the Word "dwelt" among us, literally "pitched his tent."
**Textual note:** The Hebrew word ruach means breath, wind and Spirit all at once, and the passage plays on all three. In John 20:22 the risen Jesus "breathed on" his disciples. The Greek verb there, emphysaō, is the same one the Greek Old Testament (the Septuagint) uses in Genesis 2:7 and Ezekiel 37:9.

## 3. New Heart, Clean Water, New Spirit: Ezekiel 36:25–27

**Why it fits:** God promises to sprinkle his people with clean water and remove their "heart of stone." He will give them a "heart of flesh" and put his Spirit in them. In John 3:5 Jesus tells Nicodemus he must be "born of water and the Spirit." Then in 3:10 he asks how a "teacher of Israel" doesn't understand this, which suggests Nicodemus should have known a passage like this one.
**Textual note:** This promise lines up closely with the new covenant in Jeremiah 31:31–34. Jesus invokes that new covenant at the Last Supper (Luke 22:20).

## 4. The River Flowing from the Temple: Ezekiel 47:1–12

**Why it fits:** Water flows out from under the temple and keeps getting deeper. Wherever it goes it brings life, even to the Dead Sea (47:8–9). At the Feast of Tabernacles (John 7:2, 37–39), Jesus cries out that "rivers of living water" will flow from within the believer. Revelation 22:1–2 brings the image back.
**Historical note:** Mishnah Sukkah 4:9 describes a water-pouring ceremony at the altar during that feast. That gives Jesus' words a vivid setting.

## 5. The Throne Vision and the Glory: Ezekiel 1 (with 10–11 and 43)

**Why it fits:** Ezekiel sees on God's throne "a likeness as the appearance of a man" (1:26). The Hebrew is demut kemar'eh adam. Later the glory leaves the temple and stops on the mountain east of the city (11:23). Ezekiel then sees it return from the east (43:2). In Acts 1:9–12, Jesus ascends from the Mount of Olives, which lies east of Jerusalem. Revelation 1:13–17 echoes Ezekiel's throne-room imagery.
**Textual note:** God calls Ezekiel "son of man" (ben adam) about 93 times. In Ezekiel it means "mere mortal." Jesus' title "Son of Man" draws mainly on Daniel 7:13, so the two uses should be kept apart. Jewish caution about this vision is on record: Mishnah Chagigah 2:1 restricts teaching on the "chariot" (merkavah) passage.
