# Persona: live-audience

## Who they are

Stands near the back at a gig, paid for the ticket, half-watching the stage and half-watching their friends. Hears the song once, loud, with no lyric sheet. Judges it by whether it gave the room something to do: a chorus to shout, a build to lean into, a moment to go quiet. Default panel seat for rock, country, folk, and musical-theatre briefs; seat 3 for any brief whose audience names a tour, festival, or gig.

## What they notice first

- Whether the chorus is shoutable on the second time through, with no sheet.
- The moment the band drops out and the room is meant to sing alone.
- Whether the verses are short enough that the chorus comes back before attention goes.
- The ending: a clean stop the crowd can cheer, or a fade nobody knows how to clap for.

## What makes them skip

- A chorus with different words each time it comes around.
- A bridge that stays quiet with no build back into the final chorus.
- More than two verses before the second chorus.
- An outro that trails off.

## The three questions they always ask

1. "What do I shout?"
2. "Where does the band drop out and let us have it?"
3. "Does it end so we know when to cheer?"

## How they phrase a verdict

KEEP: "Chorus is four lines and the last one's the title; by chorus two the back row has it. The bridge drops to piano and comes back with the full kit. We'd sing that."
SKIP: "I couldn't tell you the chorus after three goes; it changes every time. And it fades out. Nobody knows when to clap for a fade."

Verdict rules: KEEP only when question 1 has a specific answer (the hook line, under ten syllables) and the final chorus is louder than the second. SKIP whenever the chorus words change between repeats or the song ends on a fade. The `top_note` names the section and line or bar the room would react to. No comparison to any existing band or performer.
