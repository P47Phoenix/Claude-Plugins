# Technique: Reference-Track Language (describing a sound without naming anyone)

Producers talk in comparisons because comparisons are fast. The originality policy (clause 3) forbids them in every artifact: no artist, no title, no "sounds like", no "reference track". This file is the replacement vocabulary. Every comparison can be rewritten as a set of parameters a DAW operator can dial in; the rewrite is usually more useful than the comparison was.

## Vocabulary table

| Dimension | Words that pass | Numbers to attach |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Texture (density) | sparse, thin, layered, dense, stacked, doubled, tripled, unison, counter-line, pad under, drone | number of parts; number of vocal doubles |
| Dynamics | quiet, held back, builds, lifts, drops out, stop-time, hard-hitting, fast-attack, slow-attack, pumping, breathing | dB difference between sections; compression ratio; attack in ms |
| Space (depth) | dry, close, tight, roomy, long tail, washed, short plate, hall, spring, slapback, ping-pong, mono, narrow, wide, hard-panned | reverb decay in s; pre-delay in ms; delay note value; wet percent; pan positions |
| Tone (spectrum) | bright, dark, warm, boxy, nasal, airy, muddy, scooped, present, rolled-off, high-passed, low-passed, band-limited | bands in Hz or kHz; filter slope; boost or cut in dB |
| Rhythm (feel) | straight, swung, shuffled, half-time, double-time, four-on-the-floor, syncopated, pushed, laid back, behind the beat, quantised, loose | bpm; swing percent; subdivision; note value |
| Register | low, sub, mid, upper-mid, high, octave up, octave down, in the vocal's range, above the lead | octave numbers; note names where useful |
| Distortion / colour | clean, saturated, tape-style, tube-style, fuzzed, bit-reduced, gated, lo-fi, filtered | drive percent; sample rate; gate threshold |
| Vocal character | breathy, chesty, belted, spoken, whispered, falsetto, pitched up, pitched down, formant-shifted, vocoded, group, gang, unison, harmony in thirds | number of tracks; interval; cents of detune |
| Performance | staccato, legato, palm-muted, fingerpicked, strummed, arpeggiated, sustained, ghost notes, rimshot, brushes, mallets | note values; velocity ranges |

A phrase passes when it combines one word from a row with one number from the same row: `short plate, 0.8 s`, `wide doubles, hard-panned`, `half-time, 118 bpm`, `rolled off above 10 kHz`.

## Before and after (banned phrasing rewritten)

| Before (fails) | After (passes) |
|----------------|----------------|
| sounds like a big 80s pop chorus | gated snare with a 300 ms reverb cut hard, wide stereo synth pad, lead doubled and pitched an octave up on the hook |
| reference track: anything by <artist> | dry chesty lead, close mic, no doubles in verses; 1/4-note tape-style delay at 20 percent in the chorus |
| in the style of <band>, the drums especially | room-mic drums, slow-attack compression at 4:1 letting the transient through, snare tuned low, no sample layering |
| a <title>-type drop | kick withheld through an 8-bar snare roll, white-noise riser, one beat of silence, then four-on-the-floor with sidechained chords |
| think of a modern country radio mix | lead vocal 2 dB in front, acoustic guitar high-passed at 100 Hz, telecaster-style single-coil electric on the right, pedal-steel-style swells on the left |
| the vibe of a late-night lo-fi beat | 72 bpm, swing at 58 percent, bit-reduced drums, vinyl-style noise bed at minus 30 dB, piano low-passed at 4 kHz |
| make the bridge feel like <artist> would | strip to piano and voice, add a 2.5 s hall at 30 percent wet, bass enters bar 5, drum break on bar 8 |
| a stadium-rock gang vocal | eight unison vocal tracks, panned in pairs at 30/60/90/100 percent, low-passed at 8 kHz, 6 dB under the lead |
| warm, vintage, classic | tape-style saturation on the mix bus at 10 percent, high end rolled off above 12 kHz, 2 dB shelf boost at 150 Hz |
| EDM-festival-sized | sub bass mono below 100 Hz, lead synth in unison with 7 voices detuned 12 cents, sidechain to the kick with a 150 ms release |

The right column never names a person, a group, or a record, and a stranger could set every parameter from it.

## Era and genre words

- Allowed: a decade plus a technique (`80s gated snare`, `90s boom-bap swing`, `70s tape saturation`), a genre plus a parameter (`disco four-on-the-floor at 118 bpm`), a region plus an instrument (`west-coast-style synth lead` is fine only because it names no act).
- Not allowed: a decade plus an artist, a genre plus a band, a "the <city> sound of <year>" when the phrase is a known act's identity.

## Self-check before writing

1. Search the draft for `sounds like`, `reference`, `in the style`, `think of`, `a la`, `reminiscent`, `vibe of`, `like the`, `like a` and rewrite each hit as parameters.
2. Every capitalised word that is not a section label, a note name, or the first word of a sentence is a proper noun: remove it.
3. Every adjective of quality (`big`, `warm`, `punchy`, `lush`) gets a number or a technique word beside it, or it goes.
4. Read each section note as a stranger in a studio: can they do it without asking "like what?"
