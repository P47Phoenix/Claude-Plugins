# Genre: Folk (arrangement)

## Palette

- Core: one acoustic guitar (fingerpicked or flatpicked) or piano, lead vocal. That pair can carry a whole song; everything else is optional.
- Colour: upright or soft electric bass, second acoustic guitar in a different register or capo position, mandolin, fiddle, cello, accordion, harmonica, banjo (clawhammer feel), light percussion (brushes, shaker, foot stomp, hand claps).
- Vocal layers: one harmony in thirds or a unison double on the chorus; a group unison on a refrain is idiomatic and should be loose rather than tight.
- Room sound is part of the palette: a stereo pair of room mics at 10-20 percent is a standard instruction.

## Typical energy curve (numbers for `arrangement.md`)

| Form | Curve |
|------|-------|
| V1 C V2 C V3 C | 2 5 3 6 4 7 |
| V1 V2 C V3 C B C | 2 3 5 4 6 4 7 |
| Verse-refrain (V1 V2 V3 V4, no chorus) | 2 3 4 5 (strict_rising applies; peak last) |
| Intro V1 C V2 C B C Outro | 1 2 5 3 6 4 7 2 |

Folk curves are the narrowest: verses 2-4, choruses 5-7, final chorus rarely above 7. The lift is one instrument and one harmony at a time. With no chorus-role section, the gate applies `strict_rising`, so a verse-refrain form must climb to its last verse and never dip before it.

## Instrument entry order

1. Verse 1: guitar (or piano) and voice. Nothing else.
2. Chorus: bass and one harmony voice enter; guitar opens from fingerpicking to light strumming.
3. Verse 2: harmony leaves; one colour instrument (fiddle, mandolin, cello) enters with long notes under the voice, not fills.
4. Chorus: harmony returns; light percussion enters (brushes, shaker, or foot stomp).
5. Verse 3 or Bridge: strip to the opening pair, or hand the accompaniment to the colour instrument alone.
6. Final Chorus: all parts, group unison on the refrain line if the brief is a singalong, colour instrument doubling the melody an octave up.
7. Outro: the intro guitar figure once, or the colour instrument alone, ending on a held chord with the room sound audible.

## Production tropes

- Tempo 60-120 bpm; many folk songs sit in 3/4 or 6/8, stated in `structure.md`, and the feel word (`lilting`, `driving`) goes in the texture note.
- Lead vocal close and dry: high-pass at 80 Hz, compression 2:1 to 3:1 with a slow attack, a short room or small plate at 10-15 percent, no delay.
- Guitar recorded close with the room pair; keep the fret and pick noise; high-pass at 80 Hz; no chorus or modulation effects.
- Dynamics come from playing, not processing: `strum harder from the chorus downbeat` is a legitimate instruction.
- Mix priorities favour the words: guitar 3-4 dB under the voice in verses; colour instruments ducked by 2 dB during sung lines.
- Imperfection stays: no hard pitch correction, no quantised percussion.

## Avoid

- A full drum kit before the final chorus, unless the brief says folk-rock.
- Synth pads; if a sustained bed is wanted, use cello, accordion, or a bowed bass.
- Stacking more than two vocal layers outside a group refrain.
- Naming any act, record, producer, or instrument brand.
