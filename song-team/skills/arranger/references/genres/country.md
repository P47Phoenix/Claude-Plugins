# Genre: Country (arrangement)

## Palette

- Core: acoustic guitar (strummed or fingerpicked), electric bass, kit with brushes or a light stick pattern, lead vocal.
- Colour: single-coil electric guitar (clean, slight tube-style breakup), pedal-steel-style swells, fiddle, mandolin, dobro-style slide, piano or organ pad, banjo in uptempo songs.
- Vocal layers: one harmony a third above (or below for a lower lead) in choruses; a second harmony on the final chorus; group vocals are rare and belong only to an uptempo singalong hook.
- Two string instruments trading fills is the usual texture; three at once is crowded.

## Typical energy curve (numbers for `arrangement.md`)

| Form | Curve |
|------|-------|
| V1 C V2 C B C | 3 6 4 7 5 8 |
| V1 PC C V2 PC C B C | 3 4 6 4 5 7 5 8 |
| Intro V1 C V2 C B C Outro | 2 3 6 4 7 5 8 3 |
| Ballad V1 C V2 C B C Tag | 2 5 3 6 4 7 3 |

Country curves are narrower than pop: first chorus 5-6, final chorus 7-8, verses 2-4. The bridge is a storytelling turn, usually stripped (one below the verse before it); the final chorus's lift comes from a harmony and a fill, not from a new wall of sound.

## Instrument entry order

1. Verse 1: acoustic guitar and voice, bass enters bar 3 or 5; side-stick or no kit.
2. Chorus: full kit (brushes or light sticks), electric guitar fills between vocal lines, harmony vocal enters on line 2 or 3.
3. Verse 2: harmony leaves; a fiddle, mandolin, or steel swell takes the fills; kit continues at lower velocity.
4. Chorus: harmony returns; steel or fiddle sustains under the hook.
5. Bridge: acoustic and voice only, or electric and voice; bass re-enters at the half-way bar; one fill into the last chorus.
6. Final Chorus: everything, second harmony on the hook line, a fiddle or steel counter-line above the vocal, one changed-word moment left bare if `lyrics.md` has one.
7. Tag or Outro: acoustic guitar figure from the intro, voice on the hook once, ends on a held chord or a ritardando.

## Production tropes

- Tempo 70-140 bpm; ballads 70-85, mid-tempo 95-110, uptempo 120-140; a shuffle or train beat is stated as `swung` with a percent.
- Lead vocal dry and forward, 2 dB over the band; light compression 3:1; short plate 0.6-0.9 s; no delay in verses, a 1/4-note delay at 8-12 percent in the final chorus at most.
- Acoustic guitar high-passed at 100 Hz, panned slightly off centre; electric guitar opposite; steel and fiddle wide.
- Kit tuned low and dry; snare with a short room reverb; no sample layering.
- The hook is the loudest vocal moment, not the loudest band moment: fills stop during the hook line.
- The story turn (last verse or bridge) often drops the kit for two bars so the line is heard bare.

## Avoid

- A pad under every section; the air between instruments is the genre's space.
- Doubled lead vocals in verses; the lead is one voice telling a story.
- Electronic drums or sidechain pumping unless the brief asks for a crossover feel.
- Naming any act, record, producer, instrument brand, or plug-in.
