# Genre: Pop (arrangement)

## Palette

- Core: kick, snare, hi-hat (closed in verses, open in choruses), electric bass or synth bass, one chordal instrument (piano, electric guitar, or synth pad), lead vocal.
- Colour: synth pad, plucked synth or clean electric guitar counter-line, hand claps on 2 and 4, shaker, tambourine, sub-bass drop at the chorus downbeat, string pad in the bridge.
- Vocal layers: stereo doubles, a third-above harmony, a group vocal on the hook in the final chorus.
- Keep the chordal instruments to two at a time; pop mixes are built on space around the voice.

## Typical energy curve (numbers for `arrangement.md`)

| Form | Curve |
|------|-------|
| V1 PC C V2 PC C B C | 3 5 7 4 6 8 5 9 |
| V1 PC C V2 PC C B C C | 3 5 7 4 6 8 5 9 9 |
| Intro V1 C V2 C B C Outro | 2 3 7 4 8 5 9 4 |
| Jingle (C V1 C) | 6 5 8 |

First chorus 7, last chorus 9, verses at 3-4, pre-choruses one or two below their chorus, bridge the second-lowest number or a stripped 5. A pop outro sits at least three below the peak or the song ends on the chorus.

## Instrument entry order

1. Verse 1: chordal instrument plus bass, or a bare loop; closed hi-hat or a side-stick only; single dry vocal.
2. Pre-Chorus 1: kick and snare enter; bass starts moving; a tight double on the last line.
3. Chorus: full kit with open hi-hat, pad, doubles, claps; the hook doubled.
4. Verse 2: pad leaves, kit stays at lower velocity, shaker or a new percussion layer enters.
5. Pre-Chorus 2: tambourine and backing vocals enter; tom fill into the chorus.
6. Chorus: add a high harmony on lines 3-4 or a counter-line.
7. Bridge: strip to piano or pad and voice; bass re-enters mid-section; a one-bar drum break at the end.
8. Final Chorus: everything, group vocal on the hook, open hi-hat, optional key-centre lift stated in `structure.md` only.

## Production tropes

- Tempo 95-125 bpm, straight eighths; half-time feel reserved for a verse or the bridge.
- Kick and bass locked; the pad and the chordal synth sidechained lightly to the kick in choruses.
- Lead vocal 1-2 dB in front; presence lift at 3-5 kHz; plate reverb 0.6-1 s; 1/8-note slap delay at 10-20 percent in choruses, dry verses.
- A one-beat silence or a drum break before the final chorus; a sub drop or a reversed cymbal into chorus downbeats.
- Bright top end: hi-hat and claps present; acoustic guitar high-passed at 100 Hz when used.
- The last chorus adds one audible element the earlier choruses lacked; the listener should be able to name it.

## Avoid

- Doubling the melody with a synth in the verse.
- A bridge that is only a quieter chorus.
- More than two chordal instruments at once; more than four vocal doubles outside the final chorus.
- Naming any act, record, producer, or plug-in when describing any of the above.
