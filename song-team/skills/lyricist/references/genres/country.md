# Genre: Country

## Lyric conventions

- Story first. Verses are narrative with a who, a where, and a when in the first two lines; the listener follows a person through a place.
- Objects of work and home carry the feeling: a truck bed, a porch light, a mailbox, a jar, a fence line, a kitchen table. The emotion is never named when an object can stand in for it.
- The hook is a plain spoken phrase, often with a double meaning that the last verse reveals (the "turn" lives in the final verse or bridge and changes what the chorus means).
- Conversational grammar: contractions, dropped subjects allowed in verses ("Left the light on"), direct address in the chorus.
- Time markers are specific: half past three, late July, the second Sunday. They cost no syllables and build the world.
- The last chorus is allowed one changed word that lands the twist; state it in `SUMMARY`.

## Rhyme density

Moderate to high. Verses `ABAB` or `xAxA`; chorus `AABB` with full rhyme. Slant rhyme is accepted in verses (`home`/`alone`, `porch`/`church`) and is idiomatic; the chorus holds full rhymes. Feminine rhymes are rare. Rhyme on concrete nouns, not on verbs of feeling.

## Taboo moves

- Checklists of genre props with no story (a truck, a beer, a dirt road, a girl) in one verse; one object per line, each advancing the story.
- Naming real towns, highways, brands, teams, or people; the brief's `must_avoid[]` usually says so, and the originality policy forbids artist names in any case.
- Abstract heartbreak vocabulary (`broken heart`, `tears in the rain`) sits on the cliché list; the physical trace replaces it.
- A chorus that explains the verse. The chorus is the sentence the character would say aloud.
- Mixing narrators: a third-person verse with a first-person chorus needs the shift marked in the structure.
- Irony about the genre itself.

## Typical section lengths

| Section | Lines | Syllables per line |
|---------|-------|--------------------|
| Verse | 4 (story songs: 6-8) | 8-10 |
| Pre-Chorus | 2 | 8-9 |
| Chorus | 4 | 7-9 |
| Bridge | 4 | 8-10 |

`hook_max_syllables`: no override (default 12). Country hooks run longer than pop (8 to 11) because they are spoken sentences.

## Three original example lines

```text
I keep the ring inside a jar               (hook: 8 syllables, object stands in for the feeling)
The truck still smells like Sunday rain    (verse: a sense other than sight, a time marker)
Her name is on the mailbox still           (verse: the object tells the story)
```
