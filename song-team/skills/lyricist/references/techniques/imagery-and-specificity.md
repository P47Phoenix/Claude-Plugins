# Technique: Imagery and Specificity

Verses earn the chorus by showing evidence. A verse that tells the listener what to feel is a chorus with the hook removed. This file is the method for writing lines that show.

## The rule of one

Every verse line carries **one** concrete thing: an object, a place, an action, a sound, a number. Not a feeling, not a summary, not a comparison. The listener assembles the feeling from the evidence; that assembly is what makes a lyric land.

| Tells | Shows |
|-------|-------|
| I feel so alone in this town | The pavement does not know my feet |
| Everything reminds me of you | Your boots are by the door you left |
| Time is passing and I'm stuck | The train goes by at half past three |
| We used to be so happy here | We painted the fence a second time |
| I miss my grandmother | My grandmother kept salt in a tin |

Each "shows" line is a fact the narrator could photograph. Each "tells" line is a verdict.

## Sources of specific detail

1. **The brief.** `must_include[]` and the `## Brief` paragraph name objects (bus routes, market stalls, a photo pinned to a wall). Use them before inventing any.
2. **The central image.** `concept.md` names one; show it in a different state per section (the key in a pocket, the key in the wrong door, the key on a hook).
3. **Numbers and names of things.** Platform nine, half past three, the second coat of paint. Numbers are instantly specific and never cliché.
4. **The senses other than sight.** The kettle's hum, salt in a tin, rain on a market awning. One non-visual detail per verse keeps the world solid.
5. **Work and objects of the genre's world** (from the genre file): what people in this song's world touch every day.

## What specificity is not

- **Not proper nouns of real places, brands, or people.** `must_avoid[]` often names them, and the originality policy forbids artist names. "The market" beats a real market's name.
- **Not adjectives.** "A rusty, old, forgotten gate" is three adjectives and one noun. "A gate that only opens pulled" is one noun and one fact.
- **Not similes.** "Like a bird without a nest" is a comparison, and most comparisons are on the cliché list. State the thing itself.
- **Not clutter.** One detail per line. Two details per line is a list; the listener keeps neither.

## Building a verse from evidence

1. Write the arc beat for this verse in one phrase (`arrive unseen`).
2. List eight concrete details that prove the beat. Objects and actions only.
3. Pick four (or however many lines the meter gives). Order them so the last one moves closest to the chorus's idea.
4. Write each as a declarative sentence in the brief's `pov` and tense. Subject, verb, object. No "and then", no "because".
5. Count syllables and place stress per the contract. A detail that will not fit the meter is swapped for another detail, never padded with adjectives.
6. Check that no line explains a previous line. If line 3 says what line 2 meant, replace line 3 with new evidence.

## Verse 2 moves

Verse 2 repeats the shape of Verse 1 with the time, place, or object advanced. The narrator has done something since Verse 1. If Verse 1 is the first morning, Verse 2 is a week later. If Verse 1 is the key in the pocket, Verse 2 is the key in a door. A Verse 2 that restates Verse 1 with new adjectives is the most common structural failure a critic flags.

## Pre-chorus and bridge imagery

- **Pre-chorus:** take the last verse detail and turn it toward the hook without naming the hook. "The windows glow but none of them say stay" turns windows (evidence) into a choice (the hook's territory).
- **Bridge:** one new image from a new vantage: a future day, another person's view, a room not yet shown. No new theme; a new window on the same one.

## Checks against the gates

- Every detail line ends on a noun or verb (a content word): the final `S` beat is filled and the rhyme key is clean.
- No whole phrase from the cliché list. Stock images (a long road, a broken heart, the stars) are abstractions wearing nouns; replace with a thing the narrator owns.
- Specific lines are usually shorter; recount after every swap so the syllable count stays exact.
