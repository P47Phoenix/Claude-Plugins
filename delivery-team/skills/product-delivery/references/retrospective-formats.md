# Retrospective Formats

Comprehensive guide to sprint retrospective formats, facilitation techniques,
and follow-through patterns for Scrum Bags.

---

## Start / Stop / Continue

**Setup:** Three columns on a board. Each participant writes items silently (5 min),
then the team discusses and clusters (15 min), and votes on top actions (5 min).

- **Start:** Things the team should begin doing. Focus on concrete practices, not
  aspirations. "Start pairing on complex stories" beats "Start collaborating more."
- **Stop:** Things actively harming the team. Frame as process issues, never personal
  attacks. "Stop accepting stories without acceptance criteria" is actionable.
- **Continue:** Things working well. Recognizing these prevents regression and builds
  morale. Do not skip this column under time pressure.

**Facilitation notes:** Best for teams new to retros. Time: 45-60 min. Groups of 3-9.
Use when you need a simple, low-barrier format that produces clear action items.

---

## 4Ls: Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed For

Four quadrants encourage both emotional and analytical reflection.

- **Liked:** What went well and should be preserved. Captures positive energy.
- **Learned:** New knowledge, skills, or insights gained. Promotes a growth mindset.
- **Lacked:** Missing resources, information, or support. Surfaces unmet needs.
- **Longed For:** Wishes for the future. Distinguishes between gaps (Lacked) and
  aspirations (Longed For).

**Facilitation notes:** Time: 50-60 min. Works well for mid-project retros where the
team needs to both celebrate and course-correct. The Learned column often surfaces
insights that other formats miss.

## Sailboat Retrospective

A metaphor-driven format that makes abstract concepts tangible.

- **Island (Goal):** Draw first. What is the team sailing toward? The sprint goal,
  release milestone, or team vision. Anchors the entire discussion.
- **Wind (Helpers):** Forces propelling the team forward. Practices, tools, people,
  or decisions that accelerated progress.
- **Anchors (Impediments):** Forces holding the team back. Technical debt, unclear
  requirements, slow CI pipelines, missing skills.
- **Rocks (Risks):** Hidden dangers ahead. Not current problems but potential future
  ones. Dependencies, upcoming deadlines, known unknowns.

**Facilitation notes:** Time: 60 min. Groups of 4-8. Draw the sailboat on a
whiteboard or shared canvas. Especially effective when the team feels stuck -- the
visual metaphor helps externalize problems. Ask "What would it take to cut this
anchor?" to drive toward actions.

## Mad / Sad / Glad

Emotional categorization that surfaces team sentiment directly.

- **Mad:** Frustrations and anger points. These often reveal systemic issues. Listen
  for repeated themes across team members.
- **Sad:** Disappointments and missed opportunities. Lower intensity than Mad but
  still important. Often reveals unspoken expectations.
- **Glad:** Positive experiences and wins. Balance is critical -- do not let the
  session become a complaint forum.

**Facilitation notes:** Time: 45 min. Use when team dynamics feel off or after a
difficult sprint. Set ground rules: describe situations, not people. If emotions run
high, use silent writing first, then read items anonymously. Follow up individually
with anyone who surfaces serious concerns.

---

## Timeline Retrospective

Events plotted chronologically to identify patterns and turning points.

**Process:**
1. Draw a horizontal timeline spanning the sprint (5 min setup).
2. Each participant adds events on sticky notes above (positive) or below (negative)
   the line, positioned at the approximate time they occurred (10 min silent).
3. Walk through the timeline left to right. Identify clusters and patterns (20 min).
4. Discuss: What caused the clusters? What could shift negative patterns? (15 min).
5. Generate action items from the highest-impact patterns (10 min).

**Facilitation notes:** Time: 60-75 min. Best after long sprints or when the team
cannot remember what happened. The visual timeline triggers memories that other
formats miss. Watch for recency bias -- participants often over-index on the last
few days.

---

## DAKI: Drop, Add, Keep, Improve

A decision-oriented format that maps directly to concrete changes.

- **Drop:** Practices to eliminate entirely. Requires courage to name sacred cows.
- **Add:** New practices to introduce. Limit to one or two per sprint to avoid
  change fatigue.
- **Keep:** Practices working well that should be preserved. Explicit retention
  prevents accidental loss of good habits.
- **Improve:** Practices that exist but need refinement. The most nuanced category
  -- "improve our code reviews" needs specifics like "add a review checklist."

**Facilitation notes:** Time: 50-60 min. Use when the team has established practices
and needs to evolve them. DAKI works poorly for brand-new teams that lack shared
practices to evaluate.

## Energizer Activities

Quick warm-ups (5-10 min) before the retrospective to shift mindset.

- **One-word check-in:** Each person shares one word describing their sprint. No
  elaboration. Surfaces mood quickly.
- **Sprint in emojis:** Each person picks 3 emojis summarizing their sprint, then
  explains briefly. Light and accessible.
- **Appreciation round:** Each person shares one specific appreciation for a
  teammate. Builds psychological safety before harder conversations.
- **Energy level:** Each person rates their current energy 1-5. If the average is
  below 3, consider shortening the retro or switching to a lighter format.

## Remote Retrospective Tips

Distributed teams face unique facilitation challenges.

**Tools:** Use collaborative boards (Miro, FigJam, Mural) over text-only tools.
Visual spatial arrangement matters for clustering and voting.

**Engagement:** Turn cameras on. Use breakout rooms for groups larger than 6 -- have
pairs discuss first, then reconvene. Cold-call gently to include quiet participants.

**Async pre-work:** Send the retro format 24 hours ahead. Ask participants to add
items before the session. This reduces meeting time by 15-20 minutes and produces
higher-quality items because people have time to reflect.

**Time zones:** Rotate meeting times if the team spans zones. Record action items in
a shared document, not just the board.

## Action Item Patterns

Retrospectives without follow-through are worse than no retrospective at all. They
teach the team that raising issues is pointless.

**SMART actions:** Every action item must be Specific, Measurable, Achievable,
Relevant, and Time-bound. "Improve testing" fails every criterion. "Add integration
tests for the checkout flow by end of next sprint" passes.

**Accountability:** Each action gets exactly one owner, even if multiple people
contribute. The owner reports progress at the next retro.

**Limit WIP:** Carry no more than 3 action items per sprint. Completing 2 of 3 is
better than completing 0 of 7.

**Follow-up cadence:** Review action items at the start of each retro. Mark as Done,
In Progress, or Blocked. Blocked items get immediate discussion. Items that remain
In Progress for two consecutive retros need re-scoping or explicit deprioritization.

**Visibility:** Post action items where the team sees them daily -- the team board,
the sprint backlog, or the team channel. Out of sight is out of mind.

**Closing the loop:** When an action item is completed, name it in the next retro
and acknowledge the person who drove it. This reinforces that retros produce real
change.
