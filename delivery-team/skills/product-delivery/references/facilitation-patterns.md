# Facilitation Patterns

Techniques for running effective meetings, workshops, and collaborative sessions.
The facilitator owns the process; participants own the content.

---

## Time-Boxing

Allocate a fixed duration to each activity and enforce it.

**Strict time-boxing:** Timer is visible. When time expires, the activity ends
regardless of completion. Use for brainstorming, voting, and presentations.

**Flexible time-boxing:** Timer serves as a guide. The facilitator checks: "We have
2 minutes left -- extend by 5 or move on?" Use for discussions where cutting off
prematurely loses value.

**Handling overruns:** Capture remaining points in a parking lot and schedule a
follow-up. Never steal time from later agenda items. Always make the timer visible.

---

## Dot Voting

Rapid democratic prioritization for groups of 4-15 people.

**Setup:** Display all options. Each participant gets 3-5 dots (roughly 25% of total
options). Dots can be stacked on one item or spread across multiple.

**Voting rules:** Silent, simultaneous voting to prevent anchoring. If top items are
tied, discuss only the tied items and re-vote with 1 dot each.

**When to use:** Narrowing a large list to a short list. Not appropriate for
decisions requiring deep analysis or where expertise should outweigh popularity.

---

## Silent Brainstorming

Individual ideation before group sharing to prevent groupthink and anchoring.

**Process:**
1. State the prompt clearly and visibly.
2. Silent writing (5-7 min). One idea per note.
3. Share (round robin). No critique during sharing.
4. Cluster similar ideas using affinity grouping.
5. Discuss and prioritize clusters.

**Why it works:** Groups generate more diverse ideas when individuals think first.
Silent writing eliminates anchoring by the loudest or first voice.

---

## Round Robin

Structured turn-taking to ensure equal participation.

**Execution:** Go around the room in order. Each person has 1-2 minutes. A pass
option prevents forcing contribution. Guarantees every person speaks.

**Variation -- reverse round robin:** Start with the most junior person to prevent
seniority anchoring. Important in hierarchical cultures.

---

## Parking Lot

A visible space for capturing valuable but off-topic items.

**Setup:** A dedicated board section. When something valuable but off-topic arises:
"That is important. I am adding it to the parking lot so we do not lose it."

**Scheduled review:** At session end, review each item: assign an owner, schedule a
follow-up, or explicitly drop it. A parking lot that is never reviewed teaches
participants that raising issues is pointless.

---

## Fist-of-Five

Confidence and consensus voting on a 0-5 scale.

- **0 (fist):** I block this. Fundamental objection.
- **1:** Serious concerns that must be addressed.
- **2:** Concerns but can live with it.
- **3:** Neutral. Support the group's decision.
- **4:** Good decision, I support it.
- **5:** Fully support and will champion it.

**Threshold:** All votes 3+ to proceed. 0-2 voters must articulate concerns. Use
for Definition of Done changes, process changes, architecture decisions.

---

## Roman Voting

Quick temperature check using thumbs up, down, or sideways.

Thumbs up (agree), down (object), sideways (need more information). Faster than
fist-of-five. All vote simultaneously. If down or sideways votes appear, switch
to deeper discussion.

---

## Affinity Grouping

Organizing large sets of ideas into themes for prioritization.

**Process:**
1. Spread all items (sticky notes, cards) on a surface.
2. Silently move related items near each other (5 min). No talking prevents debate
   about categorization.
3. Name each cluster with a short descriptive label.
4. Prioritize clusters rather than individual items.

**Tips:** Items that do not fit any cluster are valid -- leave them as singletons.
Clusters larger than 7-8 items should be split into sub-themes. The naming step is
critical: a good cluster name captures the theme, not just the first item placed.

---

## Open Space / Lean Coffee

Participant-driven agenda formats for self-organizing discussions.

**Lean Coffee:**
1. Participants write topics on sticky notes (5 min).
2. Each person pitches their topic in 15 seconds.
3. Dot-vote to prioritize.
4. Discuss the top topic for 5 minutes, then thumb-vote to continue or move on.
5. Repeat until time expires.

**Open Space Technology:** For groups of 15+ in half-day+ sessions. Participants
propose and self-organize into sessions. Law of Two Feet: if not learning or
contributing, move on.

---

## Remote Facilitation

Distributed facilitation requires deliberate design beyond replicating in-person
patterns.

**Breakout rooms:** For groups larger than 6, use pairs or trios first, then
reconvene. Triples participation.

**Shared boards:** Visual collaboration tools (Miro, FigJam, Mural) as shared
artifact. Screen-sharing a single presenter's view reduces participation.

**Async pre-work:** Send agenda and prompts 24 hours ahead. Use synchronous time
for discussion, not reading.

**Engagement:** Direct questions to specific people. Poll frequently. Shorter
sessions (45-60 min) outperform marathon calls.

---

## The Facilitator Role

The facilitator manages the process. Participants manage the content.

**Neutral stance:** Do not contribute ideas, vote, or advocate. If you have a stake
in the outcome, assign facilitation to someone else.

**Intervention patterns:**
- Redirect off-topic items to the parking lot.
- Name the dynamic: "We are discussing implementation before agreeing on the problem."
- Equalize: "We have heard from three people. Who else has thoughts?"
- Surface disagreement: "We have two views. Let us name them explicitly."

---

## Common Anti-Patterns

**HiPPO effect:** The Highest-Paid Person's Opinion dominates. Counter with silent
brainstorming, anonymous voting, and reverse round robin.

**Silent participants:** Some people process internally and will not volunteer. Use
structured formats (round robin, written input) rather than open discussion.

**Bikeshedding:** Disproportionate time on trivial issues. Time-box proportionally
to impact. Name it: "15 minutes on button color -- 3 more minutes, then we move on."

**Solutioning before problem alignment:** Force explicit problem statements before
allowing solution discussion.
