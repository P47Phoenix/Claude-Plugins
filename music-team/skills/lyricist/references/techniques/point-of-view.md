# Technique: Point of View

`brief.md` sets `pov`. This file is a swap-in: a role embeds it only when a critique names point of view as the failure, and it replaces one of the role's default technique files so the allowance of two holds.

## The four options and what each buys

| POV | Pronouns | Buys | Costs |
|-----|----------|------|-------|
| First person | I, me, my | Immediacy; the listener becomes the narrator | Self-pity when the verses tell instead of show |
| Second person | you, your | Intimacy or accusation; the listener is addressed | Ambiguity about who "you" is unless an early detail fixes it |
| First plural | we, us, our | Shared history; anthems and duets | Vagueness; "we" needs a named place or act in the first two lines |
| Third person | she, he, they, a name-less role | Story distance; folk and country narratives | Coldness unless one detail is close enough to touch |

Mixed POV (first person addressing a second) is the pop default: "I" narrates, "you" is the subject. State the "you" by a concrete trace (your boots, your handwriting) within the first verse.

## Keeping POV consistent

1. Decide the narrator and the addressee before writing any line. Write them in a `# ` comment at the top of the lyric.
2. Every section uses the same narrator. A bridge is allowed to shift the **vantage** (future tense, another room) but not the narrator, unless the brief asks for a switch and the structure marks it.
3. Pronoun drift is the common failure: "I" in Verse 1, "we" in the chorus, "you" in the bridge with no one introduced. Read every pronoun in order and name who it refers to.
4. Tense travels with POV. Present tense for first-person immediacy; past tense for third-person story. Mixing is allowed only at a marked turn (the pre-chorus moving from past to present is a classic hinge).

## Choosing when the brief leaves it open

- Theme is an event that happened to the narrator -> first person, present tense.
- Theme is advice, warning, or a plea -> second person.
- Theme is a place or a community -> first plural, anchored by one shared act.
- Theme is someone else's life -> third person with one first-person line in the bridge ("I was there") when the genre allows.

## Fixing a critique that cites POV

| Critique wording | Likely cause | Edit |
|------------------|--------------|------|
| "Who is 'you'?" | Addressee never anchored | Add one trace of the addressee in Verse 1 line 1 or 2 |
| "Narrator changes in the chorus" | Chorus written as a crowd line | Return the chorus to the verse's pronoun; keep the hook's meaning |
| "Feels distant" | Third person with no close detail | Give the character one object they hold |
| "Feels self-absorbed" | First person telling feelings | Replace feeling lines with evidence (`imagery-and-specificity.md`) |

All edits obey the revision contract: same line count, same syllable count, same stresses, cited sections only.

## Original examples

```text
First person, present:    I learn the bus routes upside down
Second person:            You left your boots beside the door
First plural:             We paint the fence a second time
Third person:             She keeps the porch light on for no one
Mixed (I -> you):         I kept the stub you dropped inside my coat
```

Each line fixes its narrator in the first two words and carries one object, so the POV is audible without a pronoun lecture.
