# Technique: Narrative Arc

`concept.md` carries `arc: [start, turn, end]`. This file maps those three beats onto a section list so the song moves. It is a swap-in: embed it only when a critique names the arc (static verses, a chorus that means the same thing every time, a bridge with no turn), replacing one default technique file.

## The three beats

| Beat | What it is | Where it usually lives |
|------|------------|------------------------|
| start | A condition the narrator is in; shown, not explained | Verse 1 |
| turn | A decision, discovery, or event that contradicts the start | Pre-chorus, Verse 2, or Bridge |
| end | The new condition; the hook heard with the turn behind it | Final chorus, Bridge, or Outro |

The chorus text is usually identical each time; the **arc changes what it means**. First chorus: the hook as a complaint. Last chorus: the same hook as a promise. If the arc does not change the hook's meaning, the turn is missing.

## Mapping beats to common forms

| Form | start | turn | end |
|------|-------|------|-----|
| VCVCBC | Verse 1 | Verse 2 (first half) and Bridge (deepens it) | final Chorus |
| V-PC-C-V-PC-C-B-C | Verse 1 | Pre-Chorus 1 hints; Verse 2 commits | Bridge states it; final Chorus lands it |
| AABA | A1 | B | A3 (same words as A1, new meaning) |
| VCVC (short, jingles) | Verse 1 | Verse 2 | second Chorus |
| Story song (xAxA verses, many) | Verses 1-2 | middle verse | last verse; chorus is the refrain the story tests |

Write the beat each section carries in a `# ` comment under the tag before writing lines. The comment is the plan the editor and the critic read.

## Making the turn visible

- The turn is an **event or a choice**, never a mood change. "I decide to stay" is a turn; "I start to feel better" is weather.
- Show the turn with the central image in a new state: the key that fit no door now fits one; the photo pinned to the wall now has a second photo beside it.
- Place the turn's line at the end of its section so the chorus that follows is heard differently.
- Verse 2 advances time or place from Verse 1 (a week later, a different room). A Verse 2 set in the same moment as Verse 1 has no room for a turn.

## The bridge

The bridge is the one section allowed a new vantage: future tense ("Maybe someday"), another voice, or the end state imagined before it arrives. It introduces one new image and no new theme. Its last line hands the final chorus its new meaning. A bridge that restates the chorus in different words is the most common arc failure.

## Fixing a critique that cites the arc

| Critique wording | Edit (within the revision contract) |
|------------------|-------------------------------------|
| "Verse 2 repeats Verse 1" | Replace Verse 2's details with later-time evidence; keep meter and rhyme |
| "Chorus means the same every time" | Move the turn line to the end of the pre-chorus or Verse 2 |
| "Bridge is a third verse" | Shift the bridge to a new tense or vantage; keep its single new image |
| "No ending" | Make the last chorus's final line, or an outro tag, show the end state |

Every edit keeps line counts, syllables, and stresses; the arc is fixed by changing **which** evidence a line carries, not by adding lines.

## Original example of one arc across sections

```text
arc: [arrive with a map, choose one stop to know, the vendor says my name]
# Verse 1 (start):   I count the numbers down the street
# Pre-Chorus (hint): I practice saying hello to the day
# Verse 2 (turn):    The vendor asks my name again / And this time it comes out as mine
# Bridge (end seen): The corner shop will know my face
# Final chorus:      same hook line, now a promise kept
```
