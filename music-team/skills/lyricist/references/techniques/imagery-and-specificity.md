# Imagery and Specificity

Specific details persuade; abstractions summarise. A lyric earns trust when a listener can picture an object, place or action.

## The ladder

Move each abstract word down a rung until it is something you could photograph.

| Abstract | Mid | Specific |
|----------|-----|----------|
| loneliness | an empty house | a second mug left on the shelf |
| regret | a missed chance | a ticket still in the coat |
| hope | a better day | a porch light left on |
| time passing | the years go by | the paint peeling a layer each spring |

## Method

1. Name the feeling in one word, then ban that word from the lyric.
2. Choose one central image from the brief and keep it for the whole song (see the `concept.md` `central_image`).
3. Give each verse one new detail that sits inside the central image's world.
4. Prefer nouns you can touch and verbs that move; trim adjectives that only intensify.
5. Use a number, a time of day, a weather or a material once per verse; it anchors the scene.

## Show, then tell once

Let the verses show and the chorus tell. The chorus may state the feeling plainly because the verses have earned it.

## Replacing a cliche

When `cliche_scan.py` flags a phrase:

1. Say what the phrase means in plain words.
2. Ask what that looks like in this song's scene.
3. Write the line from the scene detail, not from the old phrase.
4. Re-check meter; a replacement often changes syllable count.

## Consistency of voice

Keep the source or brief's POV, tense and register. A plain-spoken narrator should not turn ornate in the bridge. Match the genre's `## Lyric` diction notes.

## Limits on metaphor

- One metaphor per section; extend it or drop it, do not stack.
- Do not mix domains (sea and machinery in one image) unless the contrast is the point.
- Prefer a literal image that implies a feeling to a figure that explains it.

## Names

Avoid real places, brands and people unless the brief asks for them. Do not name artists or songs anywhere. Invented, plausible names work; keep them short.

## Checks before hand-back

- Can I picture each verse?
- Does each section add something the last did not?
- Is any line a common phrase I have heard many times? Replace it.
