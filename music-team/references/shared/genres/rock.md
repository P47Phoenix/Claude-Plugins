# Genre profile: Rock

## Identity
Guitar-driven, backbeat-forward, and energetic. Riffs and a live-band feel lead the song, with direct, attitude-laden lyrics.
Keep when adapting INTO rock: a guitar riff or power-chord motor, a driving backbeat, and a raw band feel.

## Lyric
- Diction: blunt, colloquial, attitude-heavy; short punchy words, occasional slang.
- POV: first-person confession, defiance, or accusation toward "you".
- Lines: 6-9 syllables; strong stresses that land on the backbeat; fragments are fine.
- Rhyme: AABB or ABCB; slant rhyme and repeated end words are common.
- Imagery: roads, engines, fire, rain, streets, night, mirrors, small-town scenes.
- Form: VCVCBC; a solo section often replaces the second half of the bridge.
- Hook: the chorus title shouted on the downbeat; a riff can act as the instrumental hook.
- Topics: rebellion, restlessness, love gone wrong, freedom, working life, nostalgia.
- Keep verses conversational and make the chorus a chant a crowd can shout.

## Harmony
- Major: I - IV - V; I - bVII - IV; I - V - vi - IV.
- Minor: i - bVII - bVI - bVII; i - bIII - bVII - IV; i - iv - V.
- Power chords (root and fifth, no third) are the default; add sus4 and add9 for colour.
- Modal habits: Mixolydian (bVII) and minor pentatonic over a major chord.
- Cadences: bVII-I back-door, plagal IV-I, and V-I with a held final chord.
- Keys follow guitar tuning: E, A, D, G, Em, Am; down-tuned for heavier styles.
- Modulation is rare; a lift on the last chorus is possible.

## Rhythm
- Tempo: 100–160 BPM; hard styles at 140–170 BPM.
- Meter: 4/4; 12/8 for slow ballads.
- Feel: straight eighths driven by kick and snare; shuffle for blues-rock.
- Groove: kick on 1 and 3 with snare on 2 and 4; open eighth hi-hat; pushes at section changes.
- Phrases: 4-bar and 8-bar blocks; a two-bar riff repeats under the verse.
- Stop-time hits and drum fills mark section changes.

## Arrangement
- Core: electric guitars (rhythm and lead), electric bass, drum kit, lead vocal; optional organ or piano.
- Texture: verse thinner (palm-muted guitar, bass); chorus wide with doubled guitars and open cymbals.
- Arc: riff intro, restrained verse, building pre-chorus, full chorus, guitar solo of 8-16 bars, final chorus repeated.
- Production: natural room drums, overdriven but defined guitars, bass locked to kick, moderate compression.
- Vocals: chest voice, a raspy edge is acceptable; gang vocals in the chorus.

## Adaptation notes
- Convert to 4/4 straight eighths at 110-140 BPM; assign a two-bar riff as the motor.
- Reharmonize with power chords and bVII or bIII borrowing; strip 7th and 9th extensions.
- Re-fit lyrics: shorten to punchy 6-9 syllable lines, put the title on the backbeat, add a chantable chorus.
- Pitfall: a soft, over-smooth guitar tone that loses edge.
- Pitfall: crowded vocal lines with no space for riffs or fills.
- Pitfall: delaying the chorus with long intros.
