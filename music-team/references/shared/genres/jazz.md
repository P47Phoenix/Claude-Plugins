# Genre profile: Jazz

## Identity
Swung rhythm, rich extended harmony, and improvisation over a repeating chorus form. A tune is a vehicle for interpretation.
Keep when adapting INTO jazz: the swing feel, ii-V-I motion, extended chords, and space for solos.

## Lyric
- Diction: sophisticated, conversational, witty; smooth vowels that sustain on long notes.
- POV: first-person, intimate and reflective; direct address to a lover or the night.
- Lines: 7-10 syllables; irregular and speech-like, with room for rhythmic displacement.
- Rhyme: ABAC or AABA couplets; internal rhyme and wordplay are welcome.
- Imagery: city nights, rain, cafes, moonlight, smoke, small hours, urban romance.
- Form: AABA (32 bars) is the default; verse-refrain with an introductory verse also fits.
- Hook: the title appears in the first or last line of the A section.
- Topics: longing, wry love, nightlife, seasons, nostalgic city scenes.
- Sections keep to 8 bars each; the bridge shifts key area and imagery.

## Harmony
- Major: IMaj7 - vi7 - ii7 - V7; iii7 - VI7 - ii7 - V7 - IMaj7.
- Minor: iiø7 - V7(b9) - i; i - iv7 - iiø7 - V7(b9).
- Chords carry 7ths, 9ths, 11ths, 13ths; altered dominants (b9, #9, #11, b13).
- Substitutions: tritone sub (bII7 for V7), chromatic ii-V chains, diminished passing chords.
- Cadences: ii-V-I in every key area; deceptive resolutions to vi or bVI.
- Keys: Bb, F, Eb, C, Db; bridges often visit IV or III.
- Modulation by ii-V pivots, typically in the bridge only.

## Rhythm
- Tempo: 60–280 BPM; ballads at 60–80 BPM, medium swing at 110–160 BPM, up-tempo at 200+ BPM.
- Meter: 4/4; 3/4 waltz and 6/8 variants.
- Feel: swing eighths (long-short, approximately 2:1), with ride cymbal on "ding ding-a ding".
- Groove: walking bass in quarters, ride pattern on 1, 2-and, 3, 4-and, hi-hat foot on 2 and 4, snare comping.
- Phrases: 8-bar sections; 32-bar chorus; heads repeat before and after the solos.
- Anticipated and delayed chord hits (on the "and" of 4) are idiomatic.

## Arrangement
- Core: piano or guitar, upright bass, drums, lead voice or horn; optional saxophone, trumpet, vibraphone.
- Texture: open and conversational; comping stays sparse behind the melody.
- Arc: head in, solos over the changes, trade fours with drums, head out, tag ending.
- Production: warm, natural room, minimal compression, upright bass tone round and woody.
- Vocals: relaxed, behind the beat, scat or varied phrasing on repeats.

## Adaptation notes
- Convert straight feel to swing eighths; slow to 90-140 BPM; fit the song into 32 bars (AABA) or 16 bars per chorus.
- Reharmonize with maj7/m7/dominant 7 chords and ii-V substitutions; add tritone subs at cadences.
- Re-fit lyrics: relax line length to 7-10 syllables, shift stress off the downbeat, allow syllables to land on offbeats.
- Pitfall: playing extended chords but leaving straight-eighth phrasing.
- Pitfall: packing every bar with changes and losing the singable melody.
- Pitfall: omitting space for improvisation.
