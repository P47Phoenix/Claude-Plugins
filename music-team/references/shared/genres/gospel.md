# Genre profile: Gospel

## Identity
Communal, testimony-driven sacred music: call and response, emotional build, and a choir or congregation lifting the refrain.
Keep when adapting INTO gospel: the testimony voice, the build, the call-and-response, and the vamp.

## Lyric
- Diction: direct, devotional, scripture-adjacent; plain words of praise, trust, and thanks.
- POV: first-person testimony ("I", "my") or communal "we"; address to God.
- Lines: 4-8 syllables in chants, up to 10 in verses; repetition is central.
- Rhyme: loose; AABB or none; repeated phrases carry the weight.
- Imagery: light, mountains, water, valleys, chains broken, doors opened, morning.
- Form: verse-refrain, with a repeated vamp; contemporary worship uses VCVCBC with a long bridge.
- Hook: a short, repeated declaration, called by the leader and answered by the choir.
- Topics: praise, deliverance, grace, hope through trouble, thanksgiving, surrender.
- The bridge or vamp builds by repetition and rising intensity.

## Harmony
- Major: I - IV - I - V; I - iii - IV - V; I - vi - IV - V; IV - I.
- Minor: i - iv - V7; ii7 - V7 - I.
- Chords: 7ths, 9ths, add9, sus4, and walk-ups; passing diminished chords; the "amen" plagal IV-I.
- Modal habits: pentatonic with blue notes; Mixolydian touches.
- Cadences: V-I with a big held plagal tag.
- Keys: Bb, Eb, Ab, C, G; modulation up a half step or whole step in the vamp is typical, often repeated.
- Contemporary worship simplifies to four-chord loops (I - V - vi - IV).

## Rhythm
- Tempo: 60–140 BPM; slow worship at 60–80 BPM, up-tempo praise at 110–140 BPM.
- Meter: 4/4; 12/8 for slow gospel ballads and 6/8 for sway.
- Feel: swung or shuffled in traditional gospel; straight in contemporary worship.
- Groove: kick and snare on the backbeat, Hammond-style organ swells, handclap on 2 and 4.
- Phrases: 4-bar and 8-bar; vamps repeat 4 bars indefinitely.
- Tempo may accelerate in the vamp to raise intensity.

## Arrangement
- Core: piano, Hammond-style organ, bass, drums, choir, lead vocal; contemporary worship adds guitars and pads.
- Texture: begins sparse (voice and piano), grows to full choir and rhythm section.
- Arc: slow verse, build, refrain, bridge, call-and-response vamp, climax, quiet tag or hushed ending.
- Production: warm and live, big room reverb, choir stacked wide, organ and piano forward.
- Vocals: soaring lead with runs and shouts, ad-libs over the choir, tight harmonies on the refrain.

## Adaptation notes
- Convert to 4/4 or 12/8 at 70-120 BPM; add a vamp section for repetition and build.
- Reharmonize with I-IV-V and 7th chords; add plagal tags and a rising key lift.
- Re-fit lyrics: recast as testimony or praise, shorten the hook to a chantable phrase, assign call and response between leader and choir.
- Pitfall: a flat dynamic arc with no build.
- Pitfall: over-clever wordplay that obscures sincerity.
- Pitfall: removing the choir response, which loses the communal feel.
