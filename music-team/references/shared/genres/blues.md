# Genre profile: Blues

## Identity
Call-and-response, bent notes, and a repeating 12-bar cycle. A plain, sorrowful or wry statement is sung, then answered by an instrument.
Keep when adapting INTO blues: the 12-bar cycle, the AAB lyric shape, blue notes, and the vocal-instrument conversation.

## Lyric
- Diction: plain, earthy, wry; idiom and understatement over description.
- POV: first-person complaint or boast; direct address to a lover, a boss, or fate.
- Lines: 8-11 syllables; each stanza is three lines, the first repeated (often varied), the third a rhyming punch line.
- Rhyme: AAB; lines 1 and 2 repeat nearly verbatim, line 3 rhymes with them.
- Imagery: trains, roads, rain, whiskey, empty beds, crossroads, hard work, bad luck.
- Form: verse-refrain, with each 12-bar stanza as a verse; loop the cycle rather than adding a bridge.
- Hook: the repeated line itself; the title is often the first line.
- Topics: lost love, money trouble, travel, hardship, humour at misfortune.
- The turnaround in bar 12 sets up the next stanza.

## Harmony
- Major: I7 - I7 - I7 - I7 | IV7 - IV7 - I7 - I7 | V7 - IV7 - I7 - V7.
- Minor: i7 - i7 - i7 - i7 | iv7 - iv7 - i7 - i7 | bVI7 - V7 - i7 - V7.
- Chords are dominant sevenths in major blues; minor blues uses m7 on i and iv.
- Quick-change variant: IV7 in bar 2.
- Turnaround in bars 11-12: I7 - V7, or I - IV - I - V with a chromatic walk.
- Blue notes (b3, b5, b7) sound against the major chord; no resolving modulation.
- Keys: E, A, G, C, Bb, chosen for open-string guitar or horns.

## Rhythm
- Tempo: 60–130 BPM; slow blues at 55–75 BPM, shuffles at 100–140 BPM.
- Meter: 12/8 for slow blues; 4/4 with swung eighths for shuffles.
- Feel: triplet shuffle (long-short eighths); straight in modern blues-rock.
- Groove: kick on 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, ride or hi-hat in triplets; walking bass in quarters.
- Phrases: the 12-bar cycle split 4+4+4; vocal in bars 1-2, answer in bars 3-4.
- Space matters: rests between vocal phrases give the instrument room.

## Arrangement
- Core: electric or acoustic guitar, bass, drums; piano, harmonica, and horns as optional colour.
- Texture: sparse and loose; the lead instrument replies to each vocal line.
- Arc: instrumental intro cycle, sung choruses, instrumental solo cycle, final sung cycle with a tag ending.
- Production: warm, slightly gritty tone, spring reverb, live-in-the-room balance, little overdubbing.
- Vocals: expressive, cracked or growled phrasing, behind-the-beat delivery, bent pitches.

## Adaptation notes
- Convert the structure to a 12-bar cycle; set 12/8 or swung 4/4 at 70-110 BPM.
- Reharmonize to dominant sevenths on I, IV, V with a turnaround; replace diatonic chords.
- Re-fit lyrics: shape each stanza as AAB with the first line repeated and the third as the punch line, in 8-11 syllables.
- Pitfall: playing the changes too neatly and dropping the blue notes.
- Pitfall: filling every gap so the call-and-response disappears.
- Pitfall: adding a bridge or modulation that breaks the cycle.
