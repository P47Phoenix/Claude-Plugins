# Genre profile: Folk

## Identity
Story-first, acoustic, and unadorned. The song is a plain narrative or testimony carried by voice and a few strummed or picked strings.
Keep when adapting INTO folk: a clear storyline, singable melody, simple repeating chord loops, and room for the words to be heard.

## Lyric
- Diction: plain, concrete, conversational; regional or period words are welcome, abstractions are not.
- POV: first-person witness or third-person ballad narrator; direct address to a "you" is common.
- Lines: 6-9 syllables, three or four stresses; end-stopped, speech-like.
- Rhyme: ABCB or AABB in quatrains; slant rhyme is accepted; a refrain line repeats verbatim.
- Imagery: land, weather, work, travel, kitchens, roads, rivers; objects carry feeling.
- Form: verse-refrain fits best; VCVCBC works for modern writing; through-composed for long ballads.
- Hook: the refrain line or its last phrase, placed at the end of every verse.
- Topics: homecoming, labour, loss, protest, small-town portraits, seasons, migration.
- Verses carry the plot; the refrain stays emotionally constant while its meaning shifts.
- Avoid heavy abstraction, showy internal rhyme, and stacked metaphors.

## Harmony
- Major: I - IV - V - I; I - V - vi - IV; I - IV - I - V.
- Minor: i - bVII - bVI - bVII; i - iv - v; i - bIII - bVII - i.
- Mostly open triads; occasional sus2/sus4 and add9 colour; 7th chords sparingly.
- Modal habits: Mixolydian (bVII) and Dorian (major IV in minor) are idiomatic.
- Cadences: plain V-I, plagal IV-I, or an open ending on the dominant.
- Keys follow guitar shapes: G, D, A, C, E, Am, Em; a capo moves the key rather than modulating.
- Modulation is rare; a lift of a whole step on the last verse is the only common change.

## Rhythm
- Tempo: 70–120 BPM; ballads sit low, walking songs at the top.
- Meter: 4/4, 3/4 waltz, or 6/8; free rubato intros are allowed.
- Feel: straight, gently syncopated by the strum; 6/8 rolls in compound feel.
- Groove: bass note on 1 and 3, down-up strum on 2 and 4, or fingerpicked alternating bass in eighths.
- Phrases: 4-bar phrases, 8-bar or 16-bar verses, refrain 4-8 bars.
- Keep the pulse human; slight push and pull is part of the sound.

## Arrangement
- Core: acoustic guitar and voice; optional fiddle, banjo, mandolin, harmonica, upright bass, light hand percussion.
- Texture: sparse, dry, close-miked; add at most one instrument per verse.
- Arc: voice and guitar alone, instruments enter by verse 2 or 3, group refrain on the last pass.
- Production: little compression, natural room, no gated reverb, no heavy processing.
- Vocals: unforced chest voice, clear consonants, unison or loose third harmonies on the refrain.
- Dynamics stay modest; the final refrain may drop to a whisper or open to a group sing.

## Adaptation notes
- Convert meter to 4/4 or 3/4 and slow the tempo; thin the chords to open triads.
- Reharmonize toward I-IV-V and Mixolydian bVII; strip jazz extensions and passing chords.
- Re-fit lyrics to a refrain structure: pull the hook into a repeated closing line, shorten lines to 6-9 syllables, make images concrete.
- Pitfall: over-polished production that erases the intimate voice.
- Pitfall: abstract, stacked-metaphor lyrics that cannot be sung in one breath.
- Pitfall: dense chord changes that bury the storyline.
