# Genre profile: Pop

## Identity
Hook-led, polished, and built for immediate recall. Short sections, a big chorus, and a clean modern sound.
Keep when adapting INTO pop: the chorus hook, a clear emotional premise, tight repetition, and a steady contemporary groove.

## Lyric
- Diction: everyday spoken English, short words, contractions, direct emotion.
- POV: first-person "I" to "you"; a shared "we" for anthem choruses.
- Lines: 5-8 syllables in choruses, up to 10 in verses; strong stresses on beats.
- Rhyme: ABAB or AABB; perfect rhyme in the chorus, slant allowed in verses; the title often closes the chorus.
- Imagery: light, night, movement, touch, small domestic details; one central image per song.
- Form: VCVCBC; add a pre-chorus lift before each chorus.
- Hook: title phrase in the first or last line of the chorus, repeated three or more times.
- Topics: romance, heartbreak, self-belief, nights out, change, independence.
- Verse 2 must add new information rather than restate verse 1.
- The bridge flips perspective or raises the stakes, then returns to a final chorus.

## Harmony
- Major: I - V - vi - IV; vi - IV - I - V; I - vi - IV - V.
- Minor: i - bVI - bIII - bVII; i - iv - bVI - V.
- Chords are mostly triads with add9, sus, and the occasional maj7 colour.
- The pre-chorus often ends on the dominant (V or IV) to build tension for the chorus.
- Cadences: loop-based; the chorus resolves to the tonic only on its last chord.
- Keys: any, chosen to suit the singer's chest and head voice; common are C, G, A, F, Em.
- Modulation: a lift up a half step or whole step before the final chorus is a classic device.

## Rhythm
- Tempo: 90–130 BPM; mid-tempo at 100–118 is typical.
- Meter: 4/4.
- Feel: straight with light syncopation; an occasional half-time chorus.
- Groove: kick on 1 and 3 (or four on the floor), snare or clap on 2 and 4, eighth or sixteenth hi-hat.
- Phrases: 2-bar and 4-bar units; verse 8 bars, pre-chorus 4, chorus 8.
- Total length 2:45-3:30; first chorus before 0:50.

## Arrangement
- Core: drums or programmed beat, bass, synth or keys, guitar, lead vocal with stacked backing.
- Texture: verse thin and rhythmic, pre-chorus building, chorus wide and full.
- Arc: short intro, small verse, rising pre-chorus, full chorus, stripped second verse, bridge breakdown, final double chorus.
- Production: tight compression, bright top end, sidechain pump, layered vocal doubles, short instrumental hooks.
- Vocals: close and intimate in verses; belted or layered in choruses; ad-libs and harmony stacks on the last pass.

## Adaptation notes
- Convert to 4/4 straight feel at 100-120 BPM; fold sprawling sections into verse, pre-chorus, chorus.
- Reharmonize to a four-chord loop; simplify extensions to add9 or sus.
- Re-fit lyrics: pull one title phrase into a repeated chorus, shorten lines, add a pre-chorus lift.
- Pitfall: a chorus without a distinct melodic peak above the verse.
- Pitfall: verses that carry too many ideas and delay the first chorus.
- Pitfall: an over-dense arrangement that leaves no contrast between sections.
