# Genre profile: Musical theatre

## Identity
Song as dramatic scene: a character wants something, and the song moves the story. Clear diction, theatrical vocal projection, and orchestral colour.
Keep when adapting INTO musical theatre: a character voice, an objective, a dramatic turn, and intelligible lyrics.

## Lyric
- Diction: character-specific, clear, speech-rhythm; every word must be audible and meaningful.
- POV: first-person in character; the singer states a want, a fear, or a decision.
- Lines: 6-10 syllables; precise stress; strong internal rhyme and wit in comedy numbers.
- Rhyme: tight perfect rhyme, AABB or ABAB; double and triple rhymes for comedy.
- Imagery: specific to the story world; props, places, relationships.
- Form: AABA for the "ballad"; through-composed for scene songs; verse-refrain for list or comic numbers.
- Hook: the "I want" or "I am" line that returns as a refrain or button.
- Topics: desire, conflict, transformation, ensemble celebration, reprise of an earlier idea.
- The song must change the situation: start in one emotional state, finish in another.

## Harmony
- Major: I - IV - ii7 - V7; I - vi - ii - V; IV - V - iii - vi.
- Minor: i - iv - V7 - i; i - bVI - iv - V.
- Chords: triads, 7ths, secondary dominants, chromatic passing chords, diminished leading chords.
- Cadences: strong V-I with a big final held chord; deceptive cadences for surprise.
- Modulation: up a half step or whole step for the final refrain (the "key change" lift).
- Keys: chosen for character range; moves between key areas follow dramatic shifts.
- Underscore sections use pedal points and tremolo strings.

## Rhythm
- Tempo: 60–160 BPM; ballads at 60–80 BPM, up-tempo numbers at 120–160 BPM.
- Meter: 4/4, 3/4 waltz, 6/8, 2/2 cut time; metre changes between scenes are allowed.
- Feel: straight or swung per number; rubato in verses.
- Groove: oom-pah in waltzes, walking bass in swing numbers, driving quarter-note comping in uptempos.
- Phrases: 4- and 8-bar, with ritardando at climaxes and vamps for dialogue.
- Rubato verse leads into a tempo refrain.

## Arrangement
- Core: piano or pit orchestra (strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion) plus the voice.
- Texture: supports the text; orchestra swells under key lines.
- Arc: rubato intro, verse, refrain, bridge that turns, last refrain with key lift and big ending.
- Production: natural, live pit-orchestra balance; voice mixed forward for intelligibility.
- Vocals: projected, clear, with character; belting or legit tone chosen by the role.

## Adaptation notes
- Recast the source as a character's objective; add a rubato verse and a tempo refrain.
- Reharmonize with secondary dominants and chromatic approach chords; add a key change before the final refrain.
- Re-fit lyrics: sharpen to a single dramatic "want", give the singer specific props and place, tighten rhyme and add a button line.
- Pitfall: a pretty song that does not change the story.
- Pitfall: mumbled diction hidden under orchestration.
- Pitfall: generic emotion without a named character or situation.
